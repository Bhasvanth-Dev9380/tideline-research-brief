The product

The memory an enterprise can actually deploy.

Every company that has tried to give its AI access to what it knows has hit the same wall. Not "can the model find the document" — that was solved. The wall is that the moment the memory is shared, it becomes a liability: it leaks between people, it goes stale and nobody can tell, nothing can be properly deleted, and it makes the bill go up.

Tideline is the layer that removes that wall. One store where every fact knows who may read it, stays correct when the company changes, can be erased with proof, and is assembled so the shared part is paid for once. It is what Spinabot sells; Grokbot is the workspace people see on top of it.

What it does

Five things that happen in a real company.

Not features. The moments where every existing product breaks, and what Tideline does instead.

A new engineer, first week

Their agent knows the company. It does not know everything.

Today

Either the assistant is wired to a shared index and can surface a compensation thread someone forgot to lock, or it is wired to nothing and the engineer re-explains the architecture from scratch every session. Most companies pick the second and call it security.

With Tideline

Their agent reads what they may read — the engineering decisions, the incident history, the naming conventions — because the filter runs before the search, not after it. Nothing they cannot see ever enters the candidate set, so nothing can slip out through a summary, a count, or a suspiciously fast answer.

Pricing changes on a Tuesday

Everything built on it updates, or admits it no longer knows.

Today

The new price is added. The old one is still in the store, in three summaries, in the onboarding page and in the sales agent's context. It keeps being quoted for weeks. This is the failure every memory system in the field shares, measured at three percent on the public test.

With Tideline

Facts that depended on the price recompute where a rule was stated, and where none was, they close and mark themselves as needing confirmation rather than answering from a stale value. The onboarding page rebuilds itself. The sales agent says it is not current instead of quoting last quarter.

Someone leaves

One action, and the receipt to prove it.

Today

Their account is disabled and their memory sits wherever it landed. In one major product it moves to an inactive mailbox and stays discoverable; in another the docs say memory is exempt from the retention policy entirely. Facts about the person are nobody's feature.

With Tideline

Their private compartment exports to them or is destroyed with its key. Their contributions to team and company memory stay, because those went through a door and were attributed at the time. Facts about them purge along provenance, and the whole thing produces a receipt an auditor can verify without being shown any content.

Something goes wrong

"Why did the agent tell the customer that, on the third?"

Today

Unanswerable. The leading enterprise assistant's own documentation states that memory actions generate no audit entries. You can see that the agent replied; you cannot see what it believed or why it was allowed to believe it.

With Tideline

Three clocks on every fact — when it was true, when the store learned it, when that person became entitled to see it — and an append-only log of the reads. The answer is exact: this fact, from this source, believed since then, readable by them because of this membership, which changed on that date.

The AI bill arrives

The shared context is paid for once, not once per employee.

Today

Every session re-sends the company context and pays full price for it, or a compression tool rewrites the conversation each turn and destroys the provider's cache — which is how the most popular tool in that category was measured making sessions fifty-three percent more expensive.

With Tideline

Blocks are ordered from public to private, so the company-wide block is byte-identical for everyone and the provider caches it once for the organization. Private compartments sit behind a boundary nobody outside them can construct — so the cheap path and the safe path are the same path, and we report the four token classes that are actually billed.

What people touch

Three surfaces.

The agent

Nothing to learn. An agent in Grokbot, Claude Code, Cursor or a customer's own stack recalls and writes through Tideline, carrying the identity of the person who invoked it — never its own. A sub-agent inherits the caller's reach rather than the owner's, which is the single change that closes the leak channel the published study found carries nearly half of all agent leakage.

Living pages

The visible payoff, and the reason the whole propagation mechanism exists. A page is built from facts, so it carries the intersection of their labels — you only see pages assembled from things you may read — and it maintains itself: when a fact underneath moves, the page updates or marks the affected part unconfirmed. A company wiki that is accurate on a Tuesday afternoon without anyone having edited it.

The claim

What we will be able to say, and what nobody else can.

Every company in this category says the same four sentences: shared memory, permission-aware, correct over time, cheaper. The first is table stakes. For the other three, the field has a self-reported table, a documented admission that role permissions live in the customer's own code, and a set of token claims that a public cost protocol has already contradicted.

The thing worth building is not a better sentence. It is the same three claims with a number behind each one that somebody else can reproduce:

  • Correct as facts change — a score on the public exam at ordinary cost, where the field sits at three percent and one.
  • Enforced, not asserted — a leak rate per channel on the published harness, and a conformance suite anyone can run.
  • Cheaper in dollars — billed cost per solved task under the public protocol, with the four token classes reported.

The aim, in one sentence. That an enterprise can say "our AI knows how this company works" and have every part of that be true — knows what is current rather than what was said once, knows it only for the people entitled to it, can prove both, and costs less than not having it.

Brigade stays public and MIT, and the memory core it ships with keeps working. Tideline v2 is the private package that plugs into the slot Brigade already exposes. Plumbing goes upstream where everyone benefits; the protocol and the models are the company's.

The design says how these guarantees are constructed, the build says what gets written, and the evidence page sources every number quoted here.
